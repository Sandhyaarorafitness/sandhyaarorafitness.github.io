Frequently Asked Questions

Everything You Need to Know Before Starting

How much weight can I lose in a month?

Many people lose around 2–4 kg a month at a steady pace of 0.5–1 kg a week, though this varies. Early weeks often include water shifts, so waist measurements matter more than the scale.

Can I lose weight without going to the gym?

Yes. Walking, bodyweight strength exercises, resistance bands, and home dumbbell workouts all work when the plan progresses steadily and your food intake matches your goal.

How long before I see results?

Energy and sleep often improve within two to three weeks. Waist measurements usually shift in four to six weeks, with visible body changes appearing around eight to twelve weeks.

Do I have to follow a strict diet?

No. Your nutrition plan uses everyday Indian meals—roti, rice, dal, sabzi, curd, paneer, or eggs—with sensible portions and flexibility for social occasions.

What is the difference between weight loss and fat loss?

Weight loss drops total scale weight, which can include water and muscle from crash dieting. Fat loss reduces stored body fat while protecting muscle through strength work and protein.

How can I lose stubborn belly fat safely?

Spot reduction does not work. Crunches strengthen muscle underneath, while reducing visceral waist fat requires overall fat loss via strength training, daily walking, and nutrition.

Can I work on weight loss with thyroid problems or PCOS?

Often yes, alongside your doctor's medical care. Progress may be steadier and slower, so coaching focuses on strength, balanced meals, sleep, and realistic expectations.

Do you offer online weight loss coaching?

Yes. Live online coaching includes video sessions, real-time form checks, Indian meal guidance, and weekly check-ins across India and worldwide, plus in-person options in Delhi NCR.

What is the difference between a weight loss trainer and a nutrition coach?

A weight loss trainer designs and supervises your workouts, while a nutrition coach guides portions and eating habits. With Sandhya Arora, both come together in one plan.

What happens if I hit a weight loss plateau?

Plateaus are normal as body weight drops. We review daily steps, protein consistency, sleep, and workout progression together and adjust calmly using data, not crash restriction.

Why Weight Management Matters: Global and Indian Facts

The World Health Organization (WHO) reports that 2.5 billion adults worldwide were overweight in 2022, and roughly 890 million of them were living with obesity. Global adult obesity rates have more than doubled since 1990. In India, the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019–21) found that around 24 percent of women and nearly 23 percent of men aged 15–49 were overweight or obese, a steep rise from earlier rounds.

Excess body fat—especially deep abdominal fat—is linked with type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease, fatty liver, and joint strain. The encouraging news is that health benefits start early. The US Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) notes that losing 5 to 10 percent of your starting weight can improve blood pressure, blood sugar, and cholesterol. For someone weighing 80 kg, that is 4 to 8 kg—achievable steadily without extreme dieting.

Weight Loss vs Fat Loss: South Asian Thresholds That Matter

The scale measures everything together: fat, muscle, bone, water, and digesting food. Dropping scale weight fast through crash dieting often sheds water and muscle tissue, leaving you weaker with a slower resting burn. Fat loss means reducing stored adipose tissue while protecting muscle through resistance work and protein.

That is why good coaching tracks waist measurements, strength progress, photos, and energy alongside body weight. For Indian adults, reference points also differ from Western standards: Indian and Asia-Pacific guidelines classify overweight from a BMI of 23 kg/m² and obesity from 25 kg/m², because South Asians tend to carry higher visceral fat and metabolic risk at lower BMIs. The International Diabetes Federation also flags a waist of 90 cm or more for South Asian men, and 80 cm or more for women, as central obesity. Another simple rule is keeping your waist below half your height. You can check yours quickly with our Free BMI Calculator.

How Weight Loss Coaching Works in Five Stages

Every programme follows a structured sequence tailored to your schedule and health profile:

  1. Consultation and health check: We review medical history, medications, thyroid or PCOS context, sleep, stress, work hours, and everyday meals. Where relevant, we plan around your doctor's clearance.
  2. Baseline measurements: Weight, waist, hips, private photos, and simple functional checks such as chair stands or push-ups set an honest starting benchmark.
  3. A personalised plan: Strength and aerobic sessions matched to your home or gym setup, a realistic daily step target, and Indian nutrition guidance built around your household kitchen.
  4. Live coaching and accountability: Technique is corrected in real time, and regular check-ins keep you steady during busy weeks without all-or-nothing thinking.
  5. Review and adjust: Every few weeks we evaluate waist measurements, energy, and adherence to adjust workouts or portions. Plateaus are solved calmly with data, not guilt.

Exercise for Weight Loss: Strength, Cardio & Daily Steps

Exercise alone rarely produces dramatic weight loss without food alignment, yet it is vital for preserving muscle, protecting heart health, and keeping weight off long term. A balanced week includes three parts:

  • Strength training (2–3 days weekly): Resistance training using bodyweight, bands, or dumbbells protects lean muscle during fat loss. The WHO recommends muscle-strengthening activity on two or more days a week. Most clients build around squats, hip hinges, pushes, rows, and core bracing.
  • Aerobic exercise (150–300 minutes weekly): Brisk walking, cycling, swimming, or online aerobics classes strengthen your heart and increase weekly calorie burn. The WHO advises 150–300 minutes of moderate activity weekly. Notably, the American College of Sports Medicine (ACSM) reports that 150–250 minutes a week yields modest weight loss on its own, whereas exceeding 250 minutes links with clinically significant loss—showing why pairing exercise with diet works best.
  • Daily movement (NEAT): Short walks after meals, taking stairs, and standing between meetings accumulate substantial energy expenditure across a week. We set an achievable step target and raise it gradually.

If you enjoy gentler movement on recovery days, yoga training fits cleanly alongside strength sessions. For troubleshooting stalled workouts, read why you may be working out without losing weight.

An Indian Diet Plan for Weight Loss You Can Follow

You do not need to give up roti, rice, or home cooking. Traditional meals are rarely the obstacle; oversized refined-carb portions, excess cooking oil, sweetened chai, and packaged snacks usually are. A realistic Indian nutrition plan works across vegetarian, eggetarian, vegan, and non-vegetarian homes:

  • Build the plate: Aim for half the plate as sabzi or salad, a quarter as protein (dal, paneer, curd, soya, chana, rajma, eggs, fish, or chicken), and a quarter as roti, rice, or millets.
  • Anchor protein at each meal: The International Society of Sports Nutrition (ISSN) suggests 1.4–2.0 g of protein per kg of body weight daily for exercising adults to support fullness and muscle retention (people with kidney disease should confirm targets with their doctor).
  • Prioritise fibre: Vegetables, whole fruits, legumes, oats, and millets slow digestion and curb mid-afternoon cravings.
  • Trim liquid calories and ultra-processed snacks: Sugary beverages, packaged juices, biscuits, and namkeen add energy quickly. In line with WHO guidance, keep free sugars under 10 percent of daily energy and salt under 5 g a day.
  • Keep flexibility for real life: Weddings, festivals, and travel are planned around calmly rather than treated as failures. Explore our Nutrition Coaching and fat loss nutrition guide for deeper meal examples.

Belly Fat, Sleep, Stress & Hormonal Conditions

Belly fat attracts the most frustration—and the biggest myth: that crunches burn it locally. Spot reduction does not work. Core exercises strengthen muscle underneath without pulling fat from the skin directly above. Deep visceral fat around internal organs responds to sustained overall fat loss, regular strength and aerobic exercise, fibre-rich food, and quality sleep.

Sleep and stress directly influence appetite. Laboratory studies show that short sleep correlates with higher ghrelin (a hunger hormone) and lower leptin (a fullness signal), making evening cravings harder to manage. Aim for seven or more hours of sleep nightly, and consider simple wind-down breathwork from our online meditation classes if work stress drives late-night snacking. Hormonal conditions like hypothyroidism and PCOS can slow the pace slightly, yet structured strength work, balanced meals, and physician-guided care still deliver steady progress.

Common Weight Loss Myths vs Facts

  • “Skipping meals speeds up fat loss.” For most people it leads to rebound overeating later and sluggish workouts.
  • “Carbs make you fat.” Eating more total energy than you burn drives fat gain; measured roti, rice, and millets fit comfortably inside fat loss.
  • “Sweating means burning fat.” Sweat cools body temperature; hydration replaces water weight, while fat loss reflects steady weekly energy balance.
  • “Weights make women bulky.” Building large muscle takes years of dedicated training and caloric surplus; strength work creates a firmer, stronger frame.
  • “Detox teas and fat burners work.” Most rely on water loss or stimulants with weak evidence and potential side effects.

Coaching fits adults seeking sustainable fat loss: desk-bound professionals, beginners returning after long breaks, women rebuilding fitness postpartum with doctor clearance, and older adults staying strong while easing joint load.
